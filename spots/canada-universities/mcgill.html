McGill University

Why it ranks: A downtown Montreal campus and a city that stays up later put McGill on community lists. The page is unofficial, and the city's rules are not a challenge.

McGill University sits on the edge of downtown Montreal, which means the campus night and the city night are neighbours. Milton-Parc and the Plateau are the names students use for cafes, cheap dinners, and rooms that are not trying to be a resort. A mountain park is the backyard. The city around the gates is the actual draw: live music, late kitchens, and neighbourhoods that each have a different first language and a different menu.

Quebec's age of majority is 18, and on-site alcohol service can run until 3 a.m. unless the venue or the city sets an earlier hour. Montreal can change hours by by-law, so '3 a.m.' is a provincial pattern, not a promise. None of that is a reason to stretch a night. A show and a dinner in the Plateau is the version of McGill that still feels like the city in the morning. Campus events and intramurals are the traditions that do not need a bar.

The metro is excellent and it closes. Night buses cover some routes after that. Rideshare is the backup up the hill to residences or across to a friend's place. Stay with your group, watch your drink, and check the last train before the set starts. A small sip is optional. Passing is always fine.
