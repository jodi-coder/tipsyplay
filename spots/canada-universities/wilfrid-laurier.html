Wilfrid Laurier University

Why it ranks: Uptown Waterloo and a famous homecoming street put Laurier on community lists. The street party is something the city manages, not a tradition to chase.

Wilfrid Laurier sits beside uptown Waterloo, which is why the campus night and the city night blur together. King Street patios, restaurants, and rooms are a short walk from parts of campus in good weather. Students share the sidewalks with residents and with the University of Waterloo crowd from a bit further out. It feels like a college downtown because the downtown is right there.

Homecoming has included big gatherings on Ezra Avenue, a street the city and the school have spent years trying to manage. Treat that as a warning, not an invitation. The better Laurier traditions are the ones with a schedule: football, music at faculty events, orientation that does not depend on a porch. A regular night is dinner uptown and one room, then residence. You are not missing a requirement.

Grand River Transit and the ION trains help along the main corridor, and they thin out late. Rideshare is the easy link if the group is split between Laurier, a house, and a friend's place near the other campus. Stay with people you know, keep a drink in sight, and leave before a street crowd becomes the plan. A small sip is optional. Passing is always fine.
