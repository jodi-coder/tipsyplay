Chicago

Why it ranks: Neighbourhood rooms, house and blues history, and a real food city put Chicago on community lists. TipsyPlay is not citing an official index.

Chicago's night lives in neighbourhoods more than in one postcard strip. Wicker Park and Logan Square are the names friends trade for bars and restaurants. River North and the West Loop are the dressed-up, reservation version. A blues room, a house-music history that locals still argue about, and neighbourhood taverns all qualify as a night out. Downtown is for the show you bought tickets to, then you leave it.

Food is a serious competing plan: deep dish if you must, and a long list of rooms where dinner is the reason you got on the train. Summer patios and winter coats are both normal. Sports nights near the United Center or the stadiums change a whole side of town for a few hours. You can follow the crowd or you can eat in the neighbourhood you already like.

The CTA trains and buses are how the city actually moves, and the owl routes are slower than the evening ones. Rideshare is the bridge when the nearest stop is a long cold walk. Neighbourhoods change character block by block. Stay with people you know, keep a drink in sight, and head back before the wait feels like the main event. A small sip is optional. Passing is always fine.
