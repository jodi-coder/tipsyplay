New Orleans

Why it ranks: People name it for live music and late food, especially around Frenchmen Street. TipsyPlay lists it as a reputation pick, not an official rank.

New Orleans is a music city that happens to have bars, which is the right way around. Frenchmen Street is the block visitors get sent to for brass, jazz, and rooms where the band is the reason the door exists. The French Quarter is the denser tourist walk, with famous names, street noise, and restaurants that define a trip more than any single cocktail. Marigny and the Warehouse District give you other doors if the Quarter feels like too much.

Food is not a side quest. Late plates, po'boys, and a reservation you actually keep can be the whole night, with one set of music attached. Mardi Gras, Jazz Fest, and game days multiply the crowds and the scams. Those weekends reward a plan: a meeting point, a spending limit, and a decision about when to leave. Go-cups are a local habit, not a suggestion to wander forever.

Streetcars are charming and not a late-night system you should bet on. Rideshare is the reliable link between neighbourhoods, and surge pricing on big weekends is normal. Stay on lit streets with your group, keep a drink covered and in sight, and treat the Quarter's hustle as background, not a friend. A small drink is optional. Passing is always fine.
