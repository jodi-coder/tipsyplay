Best party spots: Canada cities

This order follows Uber Canada's 2026 Nightlife Index, the company's fifth annual list, as reported by Daily Hive and TodoCanada. Those reports say Uber used aggregated, anonymized Uber and Uber Eats data from September 30, 2025 through September 30, 2026. A Particle write-up of the same index describes the measure as average late-night trips between 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. It ranks ride demand, not which city throws the best party. The Montréal card is a bonus about Quebec's rules, not an eleventh rank.

  1. 1

    London, Ontario

    Richmond Row patios, live rooms, and an easy ride back when the buses thin out.

  2. 2

    Winnipeg

    Exchange District rooms, Osborne Village patios, and a winter city that still goes out.

  3. 3

    Lethbridge, Alberta

    A compact downtown night in southern Alberta, with the coulees as scenery and a short ride home.

  4. 4

    Kitchener-Waterloo

    Uptown Waterloo and downtown Kitchener, two downtowns sharing one night out.

  5. 5

    Toronto

    King West, Ossington, and Kensington give you more than one way to spend a night.

  6. 6

    Halifax

    Argyle Street, waterfront patios, and east-coast rooms that still book live bands.

  7. 7

    St. John's, Newfoundland

    George Street is a short, loud walk of rooms, with music closer to a kitchen party than a megaclub.

  8. 8

    Windsor, Ontario

    A river city with downtown rooms, a student crowd, and Detroit's lights across the water.

  9. 9

    Saskatoon

    Broadway and downtown share the night, with the river in between and a short ride home.

  10. 10

    Vancouver

    Granville, Gastown, and Commercial Drive are different nights. The SkyTrain will not wait forever.

Montréal, a bonus note

Montréal is not one of the ten ranked cities. It is here because Quebec's rules differ from the provinces above, not because this page is scoring its parties.

The age of majority in Quebec is 18. A permit holder generally may not sell or serve alcohol to a minor, and a bar permit generally may not admit a minor. Narrow exceptions exist, including a parent with a minor on a terrace before 10 p.m. That is a legal framework, not an invitation to start early.

A permit to serve alcohol for drinking on site may be used from 8 a.m. until 3 a.m. the next morning. Montreal can set different hours by by-law, and any room can close earlier, so a 3 a.m. last call is the provincial pattern rather than a promise at every door. The metro does not run all night. Plan a rideshare. This note is general information, not legal advice.

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.
