Vancouver

Why it ranks: Vancouver rounded out Uber's 2026 top ten. A famous food city can still rank tenth when the measure is late-night ride demand, not reputation.

Vancouver's reputation is food, neighbourhoods, and a downtown that looks busier in photos than it feels on a random Monday. Granville Street is the obvious nightlife strip, with big rooms and a crowd that can turn loud. Gastown is the cobblestones-and-restaurants version. Commercial Drive and Main Street are where people go when they want dinner and a smaller bar instead of a lineup. Pick one. The distances look short on a map and are not, once you are actually walking.

Shows at arenas and theatres dump a lot of people onto the sidewalk at once. Patios have a long season by Canadian standards, and late kitchens are a reason to stay out even if you are skipping alcohol. You can build the whole night around a reservation and a DJ set. The mountains are scenery for another day, not a midnight hike.

The SkyTrain is excellent until it closes, which is earlier than a lot of visitors expect. Night buses cover some routes after that. Rideshare is the normal way between downtown and a neighbourhood stay once the trains stop. Stay with your group, watch your drink, and do not count on a casual walk across the bridges late. A small sip is optional. Passing is always fine.

Order follows Uber Canada's 2026 Nightlife Index, as reported by Daily Hive and TodoCanada. It tracks late-night ride demand, not a best-party prize.

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.
