What is Hot Take Court?

Hot Take Court puts one player on the stand with a silly opinion. Everyone else votes guilty or not guilty. It is a medium-energy party game for adults that rewards quick wit more than volume. TipsyPlay runs the prompt, the defense window, and the vote on every phone so the courtroom stays organized.

Best with 3–8 players. No props required — just phones and a willingness to defend pineapple on pizza like it is case law.

Setup

  1. Host a room. Friends join with the code.
  2. Pick who stands first, or let the app rotate.
  3. Agree that votes are playful, not personal.

Rules

  1. The defendant gets a short window to defend the hot take.
  2. The group votes guilty or not guilty.
  3. A guilty verdict means one small optional sip — or you lose a point.
  4. Nothing stacks. The next case starts fresh.

Variations

  • Expert witness: one friend must argue the opposite side for thirty seconds.
  • Appeal: loser of the vote picks the next topic.
  • Quiet court: no interrupting during the defense.

Hot Take Court works best when the table agrees the verdict is comedy, not a character judgment. Rotate defendants often so one person is not always on the stand. It is one of the strongest no-props games for adults on TipsyPlay when you want debate energy without a deck.

Non-alcoholic option

Guilty means you lose a point. Soft drinks and water welcome. Of legal drinking age only — house code.

Pair with Who's Most Likely for more group votes.

FAQ

Can I play without drinking?

Yes. A guilty verdict may be one small optional sip, or you lose a point. Water and soft drinks are welcome. You may decline both a drink and any alternative — no replacement action. If the screen only offers an action you refused, pause or stop.

How many people can play?

The live listing is 3–8 players.

Does a guilty verdict always cost a point?

No. It depends on the mode. In the usual game, a guilty verdict can be one small optional sip or a point, and you may decline both — you do not owe a replacement action. If the host turns on Non-alcoholic mode, a guilty verdict takes a point off the person on the stand. Alcohol is never required either way.

Is there a way to sit out a vote?

Yes — there is a Pass control for the vote. That is a vote control, not a drink-decline button. House policy still lets you decline a drink and any alternative.
