How King's Cup works on TipsyPlay

King's Cup is a classic card drinking game for adults. On TipsyPlay, one person hosts a room and everyone else joins with a short code on their own phone. There is no shared deck to pass around — each draw happens in the app so every player sees the same card and the same rule.

When it is your turn, tap to draw. The card shows a rank, a suit, and a clearly named rule. You may take one small optional sip or the named alternative on the screen, or decline both. Water counts. Nothing stacks from one card into the next, and the cup at the top of the table only counts kings — it is not a drink.

Fives and sixes can match a player group you pick when you join (everyone, guys, or girls). Active rules such as mates and house rules stay visible so the table stays fair. When the fourth king lands, the round ends with a final-king moment, then the host can deal a new deck at the same table.

Non-alcoholic option

Use a pose, a light dare, or a point instead of a sip. You may decline both the sip and the alternative — you do not owe a replacement action. If the screen only offers an action someone has declined, pause or stop instead of recording a choice they did not make. King's Cup on TipsyPlay is built so drinkers and non-drinkers can play the same card drinking game together.

Play responsibly

You must be of legal drinking age where you are. Keep sips optional and small. No chugging, no races, and never drink and drive. Plan a ride before you start. Read the full house code.

Optional sips, no stacking

King's Cup on TipsyPlay is still a card drinking game for adults — just not the version where drink penalties pile up or the cup is something to finish.

House policy: every card is opt-in. You may take one small optional sip, use the named alternative, or decline both. You do not owe a replacement action. Drink penalties do not stack from one card to the next. There is no chugging and no race to empty a cup. The on-screen cup only counts kings; it is not a drink.

How the screen actually works: the turn moves on when someone records a sip or the alternative. There is no separate Pass button and no non-alcoholic mode switch. If a person declines both actions, pause or stop. Do not tap a button that records a choice they refused.

You must be of legal drinking age in your location. Water and soft drinks count. See the house code, or try Card Lottery for a simpler red-or-black round.

FAQ

Can I play without drinking?

Yes. Each card shows one small optional sip or a named alternative (a pose, a light dare, or a point). Water counts. You may decline both the drink and the alternative — you do not owe a replacement action. If the screen only offers actions someone has declined, pause or stop instead of recording a choice they did not make.

Is there a Pass button or a non-alcoholic mode switch?

The turn moves on when someone records a sip or the alternative. There is no separate Pass button and no non-alcoholic mode switch. You may still decline both a sip and the alternative. If the screen only offers an action someone has declined, pause or stop — do not tap a completion button for them.

Does anyone drink the cup when the fourth king lands?

No. The on-screen cup only counts kings. It is not a drink. Drink penalties do not stack from one card into the next. There is no chugging and no race to finish a cup.

Do I need a physical deck?

No. Each draw happens in the app so every player sees the same card and the same rule. Friends join with a short code on their own phones.

How many people can play?

The live listing is 2–10 players.
