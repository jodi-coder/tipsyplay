How to Host an Alcohol-Free Party Game Night

Prepared 7 Nov 2026 · Adult game-night guide

A good game night needs a shared joke, not a shared drink. Make alcohol-free the plan from the invitation onward so nobody has to negotiate their place at the table.

Editorial suggestions: The table activities in this guide are ideas for your own night. They are not TipsyPlay modes, settings, or in-app controls.

Before guests arrive

This flexible running order is for four to eight adults. Change activities when the group wants to; there are no deadlines for eating or drinking.

Say: “Snacks, soft drinks and silly games. Join in, watch or pass whenever you like.” Ask about food needs privately. Set out water and clearly labelled drink choices; nobody needs to explain their choice. Leave a comfortable seat outside the playing circle.

Put paper and pens beside the snacks. Keep music low enough that an ordinary speaking voice works. You do not need prizes or a packed schedule.

Start with Menu Mix-Up

This first activity is an editorial suggestion for the table, played with paper you already have.

Give everyone a scrap of paper. Each willing player invents a completely fictional café item: cloud toast, pocket soup, whatever sounds ridiculous. Go around once, letting each person name their item and explain it in one sentence. A pass simply moves the turn along.

On a second circuit, volunteers combine two items into a café special. Ask the original inventors before using their ideas. Finish after that circuit. There is no winner and no penalty.

Move into a shared story

For a phone-based middle act, Story Chain shows one shared prompt on every phone. Each player types one short sentence or taps Pass. Lines show up as they are locked in. The public page lists 3–10 players, and you can start with two phones. The host picks 5, 10, 15, or 20 rounds before the start. A phone that was not already in the room cannot join after the host leaves the lobby. The host can tap Restart with same game for a fresh code. Keep the subject imaginary rather than mining guests' personal lives.

For a paper-only version, write “Our café received a mysterious delivery.” Each person adds one spoken sentence, or passes. After everyone has had two opportunities, a volunteer supplies the ending. This paper circle is an editorial suggestion, not a Story Chain setting.

Pause before picking anything else

Offer a snack break and ask: “More story, something different, or just chat?” Silence is not a yes. If people want to stop, the evening is already working.

Browse the game directory only if the group wants another activity. Read the chosen game's player count and on-screen alternative before starting.

Finish without a finale nobody asked for

Thank the storytellers and the spectators equally. Share any photos only with permission. Opting out never needs a reason. The responsible-play house code is the baseline for the night, including an alcohol-free table.

Play responsibly

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.

Every game is opt-in. Passing is always allowed, without explanation or a replacement task. Water, soft drinks and alcohol-free play are full options. No drinking races, chugging, pressure to finish a drink, or penalties that stack from one turn to the next. If you choose alcohol, keep any sip small and optional; breaks never require a drink. Never drink and drive. Plan a ride home before you start.
