10 Best Card Drinking Games for Adults (With Non-Alcoholic Options)

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · ~8 min read · Games for adults

Card drinking games are still the easiest way to turn a kitchen table into a party — as long as every round stays optional, small, and kind. Here are ten beginner-friendly picks, each with a clear sip-or-swap so non-drinkers can play for real.

How to choose a card drinking game

Start with the size of your group and the energy you want. Pairs and tiny hangouts love a quick red-or-black flip. Bigger tables usually want a shared deck rhythm — draw, read, react — so nobody sits out for long. If half the room is not drinking, pick games that already ship with points, poses, or tallies. That is the TipsyPlay house style: drinking games and party games for adults where water is a first-class choice.

Skip any version that stacks penalties, races empties, or pressures people to finish a cup. A good card night is memorable because of the rules and the roasting — not because someone felt cornered.

The 10 best card drinking games

  1. 1. King's Cup

    The crowd classic. Players draw from a deck (or a phone deck), match the rank to a rule, and the table reacts. On TipsyPlay, each phone joins with a code, every card shows one clear action, and the on-screen cup only counts kings — it is not a drink vessel.

    Sip-or-swap: one small optional sip or a pose, dare, or point. Pass anytime.

    Play King's Cup

  2. 2. Card Lottery

    No physical deck required. Everyone locks red or black; the phone flips. Wrong guesses take one optional sip — max one per round — or a tally mark. It is one of the fastest card drinking games for adults who want something light between snacks.

    Sip-or-swap: wrong guess → one small sip or a tally. Non-alcoholic mode races points only.

    Play Card Lottery

  3. 3. Rule Maker

    Cards invent silly table rules instead of building a heavy king's-cup pile. Slip once and you take a single optional sip or lose a point. Nothing escalates. Great when you want structure without a marathon deck.

    Sip-or-swap: one slip → one sip or one point. No stacking.

    Play Rule Maker

  4. 4. Ride the Bus (home rules)

    A quieter end-of-night card drinking game: one player answers color, then suit, then range while the table watches. Keep stakes tiny. If your group only knows the harsh bar version, rewrite it tonight — one miss equals one optional sip, then the next friend rides.

    Sip-or-swap: miss a step → sip or switch seats / tell a one-line story.

  5. 5. Higher or Lower

    Flip a card, guess whether the next is higher or lower, continue until a miss. Cap the chain. This is a perfect warm-up before heavier games for adults and needs almost no teaching.

    Sip-or-swap: wrong guess ends the run with one sip or a stretch / clap streak.

  6. 6. Simple Red / Black

    Even simpler than Card Lottery: call a color before each flip. Ideal for two people on a couch. Rotate dealers every five cards so nobody monopolizes the deck energy.

    Sip-or-swap: miss → sip or lose a poker-chip token from a shared pile.

  7. 7. Categories with a deck

    Draw a suit to pick a category theme (hearts = relationships, spades = work, etc.), then play a Category Countdown-style naming burst. Combining cards with a timer keeps drinking games from feeling like only sips.

    Sip-or-swap: freeze on the timer → sip or animal impression (TipsyPlay's Category Countdown alt).

    Try Category Countdown

  8. 8. Mate / Thumb / Mini rules pack

    Deal a short custom pack: mate cards, thumb cards, and story cards only. Shorter decks prevent fatigue. TipsyPlay's King's Cup already surfaces mates and house rules on screen so newcomers are not lost.

    Sip-or-swap: break a mini-rule → sip or high-five everyone / refill water.

  9. 9. Truth cards + Two Truths

    Shuffle conversation prompts into a deck, then alternate with a round of Two Truths & a Twist. Card drinking games do not have to be only mechanical flips — mix in stories so the night feels human.

    Sip-or-swap: fooled by a fib → sip or hand over a point.

    Play Two Truths & a Twist

  10. 10. Hybrid: cards, then phones

    Start with a physical deck for texture, then jump into TipsyPlay rooms for Never Have I Ever, Who's Most Likely, or Sketchy Guess when people want prompts without shuffling. The best games for adults often mix formats so energy resets.

    Sip-or-swap: whatever the phone screen lists — always one optional action, never a stack.

Hosting tips for card drinking games

  • Explain sip-or-swap before the first draw.
  • Put water on the table first. Soft drinks count.
  • Cap rounds. A shorter game beats a fuzzy one.
  • If someone looks uncomfortable, retire the rule — no debate.
  • Use phone rooms when the deck is sticky, lost, or incomplete.

Quick FAQ about card drinking games

Do card drinking games work without alcohol? Yes. Every game in this list includes a sip-or-swap. Points, poses, tallies, and challenges are complete ways to play — not consolation prizes.

What if only two people want to play? Start with Card Lottery, Simple Red/Black, or Higher or Lower. TipsyPlay also filters games for 2 on the main games list.

How do we keep the night beginner-friendly? Teach one rule at a time, say the non-alcoholic option out loud, and stop while people still want one more round. Phone rooms help when the physical deck is incomplete.

Where can we play right now? Open TipsyPlay in the browser, host a room, and share the code. No app download. Try King's Cup or Card Lottery first if you want card drinking games with synced phones.

Play responsibly

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.

Know your limits. Sips are optional and small. There is no chugging, no races, and no pressure to finish a drink. Every game above works with non-alcoholic drinks, points, dares, or finger-folds.

Never drink and drive. Plan a ride home before you start.
