Story Game Prompts for Adults: Start Small, Get Weird

Prepared 7 Nov 2026 · Adult game-night guide

“We need a story” is a surprisingly hard prompt. “The lift stopped at a floor labelled Tuesday” gives the next person somewhere to go.

Editorial suggestions: The table activities in this guide are ideas for your own night. They are not TipsyPlay modes, settings, or in-app controls.

A paper-free home version

Start with one strange detail, keep the characters fictional and make ending the tale part of the rules. You don't need impressions, personal confessions or a drink in hand. This spoken circle is something you run yourselves, not a TipsyPlay mode. When you want prompts on phones, Host Story Chain.

Gather three to eight willing adults. Choose a starter below and a direction around the circle. Each person adds one sentence or passes. Repeat the previous sentence whenever someone needs a reminder.

Complete two circuits, then invite a volunteer to end the story in one sentence. If nobody wants the ending, the host says, “And that is where we'll leave them.” There are no points or penalties. Stop, or choose a fresh starter, only after checking that everyone wants more.

Six doors into a silly story

  • The lift stopped at a floor labelled Tuesday, where someone was watering a cloud.
  • A very small dragon applied to look after the office plants.
  • The museum's newest exhibit politely asked for a lunch break.
  • A lighthouse keeper received a parcel addressed to the moon.
  • Every umbrella in the café opened when the pianist played one particular note.
  • The town's lost-property box contained tomorrow's weather.

These starters are intentionally open. You can solve the mystery, ignore it or turn it into a cooking disaster. Don't “correct” another person's contribution unless it crosses an agreed boundary.

Rescue a stalled turn

Offer a choice, not a countdown: “Does a visitor arrive, or does something change color?” The player can take either, suggest something else or pass. Avoid piling several new rules onto a nervous storyteller.

If the plot gets tangled, let the next sentence begin “Meanwhile…” and follow a simpler scene. Keep jokes about fictional situations, not guests' bodies, identities or private lives.

Give the ending a job

Bring back one object from the opening, then let a character make a small decision. The dragon keeps one plant. The moon sends a thank-you note. A tidy little callback beats another twenty minutes nobody requested.

Want prompts on your phones?

Story Chain shows a shared prompt and asks each phone to type one sentence or pass. Submitted lines show up for the room. Story Chain is for 3–10 players. The host picks 5, 10, 15 or 20 rounds. A non-pass sentence adds a point on the scoreboard. The two-circuit ending, rescue choices and starters above are home rules you run yourselves, not built-in settings.

Play responsibly

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.

Every game is opt-in. Passing is always allowed, without explanation or a replacement task. Soft drinks and alcohol-free play are full options. No drinking races, chugging, pressure to finish a drink, or penalties that stack from one turn to the next. If you choose alcohol, keep any drink small and optional; breaks never require a drink. Never drink and drive. Plan a ride home before you start.
