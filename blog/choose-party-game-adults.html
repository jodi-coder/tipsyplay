How to Choose a Party Game for Your Group Tonight

Prepared 7 Nov 2026 · Adult game-night guide

Picking the game can become the longest game of the night. Cut through the scrolling with four questions: who's playing, how much energy is available, what equipment is welcome, and what should stay off the table?

Editorial suggestions: The table activities in this guide are ideas for your own night. They are not TipsyPlay modes, settings, or in-app controls.

Choose a fit for tonight

You are choosing a fit, not crowning a single best party game for every adult group. The tiny warm-up later in this guide is a home activity you run yourselves. It is not an app mode, and it does not change any in-app setting. If you already know you want a simple phone start, Host Card Lottery.

1. Count willing players, not guests

Ask who actually wants to play now. Spectators don't become players just because an advertised minimum needs one more person.

For two willing players, Card Lottery can start. Each phone locks red or black, or passes, and no physical deck is required. It is for 2–8 players. The score counts a correct color. Pass skips the colour guess; it is not a drink button. Non-alcoholic mode changes the on-screen instruction to “points only, no drinking.” The score is not a tally of misses.

For a group that wants words on their phones, Story Chain shows a shared prompt and a sentence box. Story Chain is for 3–10 players. Don't treat a home storytelling circle as an app mode. Story Chain uses typed sentences in a fixed number of rounds, not an open-ended spoken chain.

2. Match the energy, not the invitation

A group that arrived ready for a big night might now want a quiet sit-down. Ask: “Talky, cards, or a break?” Include the break as an equal option.

Choose turns without time pressure if people want room to think. If nobody wants to learn a new system, the host can offer a tiny home warm-up: name a fictional café and invite one imaginary menu item from each person. Go around once, allow passes and stop after everyone has had an invitation. No points or consequences. That warm-up is a home activity, not a TipsyPlay mode.

3. Check equipment and rules before committing

If phones suit the room, open the selected game's page together and then look at the play screen before you explain it. King's Cup uses a code so each person joins on their own phone. A drawn card shows a named rule. The cup on screen counts kings and is not a drink. Moving on is a button for one small drink or a button for the alternative. Tap Pass to skip. If someone declines both, pause rather than tapping a completion button for them.

Play needs a connection. Don't describe a game as offline, one-phone or connection-free unless you have checked that specific screen.

4. Agree on comfort before the first turn

Keep personal disclosures optional. Read the non-alcoholic alternative aloud. If it also feels awkward, you may decline both the drink and the alternative, without explanation. No accumulating penalties, drinking races or obligation to finish a session.

Try a short agreed block of play, then check whether to continue. Use the controls that are actually on the screen. A suggestion in this guide is not a hidden setting.

Play responsibly

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.

Every game is opt-in. Passing is always allowed, without explanation or a replacement task. Soft drinks and alcohol-free play are full options. No drinking races, chugging, pressure to finish a drink, or penalties that stack from one turn to the next. If you choose alcohol, keep any drink small and optional; breaks never require a drink. Never drink and drive. Plan a ride home before you start.
