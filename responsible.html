Responsible play: the TipsyPlay house code

TipsyPlay is about laughs, not quantity. Play with a cocktail, a soft drink, water, or no drink — alcohol is never required.

You must be of legal drinking age in your location.

Non-alcoholic play is welcome. Water, a soft drink, or no drink at all is a full way to take part.

The house code

These are rules for the people at the table. They are not a list of buttons, and the games do not all work the same way.

House policy. Every turn is opt-in. Pass any time, without explanation. You may decline both a drink and any alternative — you do not owe a replacement action.

Some screens have a Pass control and some do not. Declining is still allowed when the button is missing. Do not push anyone to drink, to do a pose, or to have someone tap a button that records an action they refused.

Where a game mentions a sip, keep it one small optional sip. Prefer water or skip the drink entirely, and do not carry penalties into the next round. No chugging, no drinking races, and no pressure to finish a drink.

On Never Have I Ever and Who's Most Likely, the countdown is for the answer or the vote. Those games do not time a sip. A timer in another game, such as naming or drawing, is still not a reason to race a drink.

This page is not a medical or safety guarantee. The app does not measure what is in a cup, and it does not stop someone from pouring more.

No pressure, ever. Never push anyone to drink, share a secret, or keep playing.

Water and food on the table. Set them out before the first round and leave them there. That is a job for the host. The games do not all show a water reminder.

Never drink and drive. Plan a ride, a designated driver, or a place to stay before you start.

How the current games actually work

Rules pages mention non-alcoholic play. That sentence is not always a control, and some page descriptions promise a Pass button or a tally the play screen does not have. Use the buttons on the screen, and use the house policy when you want to decline.

Split Decision, Vibe Checkpoint, and Chaos Crew score points. Each has a Pass control for that game's pick, and the effect changes by step. It is not a drink button. After a reveal, a line of text offers an optional small sip, a wave, or nothing. There is no button for those three choices.

Cup Toss Challenge scores a hit as one point. Its on-screen instruction says nobody drinks from the game cups. The buttons are Hit and Miss. There is no Pass button and no sip button. The app does not check what is in a physical cup.

King's Cup shows one small sip or a named alternative on the card. The turn moves on with "We took one small sip" or "We did the alternative." There is no Pass button, and there is no non-alcoholic mode switch. The on-screen cup counts kings, and the dare list says the cup does not hold a drink. A point on the scoreboard goes up as cards are shown. That number is not a count of drinks. If someone declines both the sip and the alternative, leave it. Do not tap either button to record an action they refused. The card can stay up.

Card Lottery offers Red, Black, and Pass for the color guess. Pass skips that guess. It is not a separate "no drink" button. A correct guess scores one point, and those points stay on the board from round to round. The app does not keep a separate tally of misses. Any sip follows the house rule above: one small optional sip, not a penalty saved for later.

Never Have I Ever, Who's Most Likely, Category Countdown, and Sketchy Guess do not put a Pass button on the drink. A rules page can still say that passing is fine. That is the house policy, not an extra control.

Where a lobby has a Non-alcoholic mode checkbox, it starts off. It changes the instruction line. In Hot Take Court it can also take a point off a guilty verdict. It does not decline a drink for you. Card Lottery's checkbox does not create a miss tally. Cup Toss is scored in points either way.

Tips for hosts

Say the house policy out loud. Before the first round, tell everyone they can decline a drink and any alternative, with no explanation and no replacement.

Agree on house rules first. Card Drinking Games: Set Friendly House Rules Before You Deal is a place to start. The buttons in each game still differ.

Pick a game that fits the room. See How to Choose a Party Game for Your Group Tonight, or plan How to Host an Alcohol-Free Party Game Night.

Make stopping easy. End after a set number of rounds, and check in before starting another.

Need help?

If you or someone you know is struggling with alcohol, talk with a doctor or a local health service. TipsyPlay does not give medical care, counseling, or emergency help. In a medical emergency, call your local emergency number.

In Canada, the Canadian Centre on Substance Use and Addiction publishes Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health. That page is health guidance for people living in Canada. It is not a helpline, and it does not list a crisis phone number.

In the United States, the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357 (1-800-662-HELP) is a free, confidential, 24/7, 365-day-a-year treatment referral and information service in English and Spanish. SAMHSA's own FAQ says it does not provide counseling. TTY: 1-800-487-4889. This line is not emergency help. For a crisis in the U.S., the same SAMHSA page lists the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline as a separate line.

Sources checked 9 Nov 2026 from the primary pages named above. SAMHSA FAQ: "Do you provide counseling? No, we do not provide counseling." The phone on that page is 1-800-662-HELP (4357).
